Before you start: derivatives and integrals

If you have never seen calculus, these two ideas are the foundation of the whole book. Here they are explained from scratch, with a worked example step by step.

What is a derivative?

The derivative measures how fast something changes. If f(x) is the position of a car, its derivative is the speed. Graphically, it is the slope of the curve at a point.

Definition

f′(x) = limh→0 f(x + h) − f(x)h

In words: we take a tiny step h, measure how much the function changed and divide by the step. Then we make the step as small as we want (h → 0).

Example: derivative of f(x) = x² using the definition

  1. We compute f(x + h) = (x + h)² = x² + 2xh + h²
  2. Subtract: f(x + h) − f(x) = 2xh + h²
  3. Divide by h: 2x + h
  4. Let h → 0: f′(x) = 2x

The shortcut: the power rule

if f(x) = xⁿ  ⟹  f′(x) = n · xⁿ⁻¹

"Bring down" the exponent to multiply and subtract 1 from it. For example:

  1. f(x) = 3x² + 5x
  2. f′(x) = 3·2x + 5·1 = 6x + 5
  3. At x = 2 the slope is 6·2 + 5 = 17

And AI? A neural network learns by computing derivatives of the error: they tell it in which direction to move each parameter to make fewer mistakes. That is gradient descent (Part I).

What is an integral?

The integral is the inverse operation of the derivative. It also computes the area under a curve, that is, it "adds up" infinitely many tiny pieces.

Indefinite integral (the antiderivative)

∫ xⁿ dx = xⁿ⁺¹n + 1 + C   (n ≠ −1)

Add 1 to the exponent and divide by the new exponent. C is a constant, because the derivative of any number is 0.

  1. ∫ (6x + 5) dx = 6·x²2 + 5x + C = 3x² + 5x + C
  2. Check: if we differentiate 3x² + 5x we get back 6x + 5 ✔

Definite integral (the area)

∫ab f(x) dx = F(b) − F(a)

where F is the antiderivative of f. This is the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.

Example: area under x² between 0 and 2

  1. Antiderivative: F(x) = x³3
  2. Evaluate at the top: F(2) = 83
  3. Evaluate at the bottom: F(0) = 0
  4. Subtract: ∫02 x² dx = 83 ≈ 2.67 square units

And AI? In probability, the area under a density curve is a probability. Integrals are behind probabilistic models and statistics (Part III).
